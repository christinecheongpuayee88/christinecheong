Micro-Interventions Library

Nine fast, AI-generatable teaching moves across three levels — Clarify, Apply, Challenge — that a cohort sensing report can recommend by name and an instructor can run on the spot.

MVP principle: every move on this page is chosen because it's very fast for AI to generate and very easy for an instructor to deploy without changing the lesson plan. No new slides, no rewritten exercises — just a short prompt the instructor reads out or drops into the room.
LevelPurposeQuick-win examplesTypical time
🔴 Clarify Check / reinforce understanding Quick quiz, misconception check, worked example 2–5 min
🟢 Apply Practise in context mini case, Think–Pair–Share, different context 5–10 min
🔵 Challenge Deepen reasoning / judgement what-if scenario, multiple perspectives, challenge assumptions 5–10 min

🔴 Clarify

Check or reinforce understanding of the concept just taught — fast enough to run the moment a checkpoint reveals a gap.

Quick quiz2–3 min

One AI-generated multiple-choice question on the concept the cohort is weakest on, with a short explanation revealed after the answer.

Ready-to-run example
Quick Check · 3 min

Which statement best explains seasonal differencing? A–D choices → reveal answer → short AI explanation.

Misconception check2–4 min

Present a plausible-but-wrong answer or statement and ask the cohort to spot what's wrong and why — surfaces the exact misconception the data flagged.

Ready-to-run example
Spot the Misconception · 3 min

"A rising ACF that decays slowly means we should add more MA terms." What's wrong with this statement, and what should we look at instead?

Worked example4–5 min

AI walks through one complete example of the concept step by step, using the cohort's actual data or a close variant of it.

Ready-to-run example
Worked Example · 5 min

Step through selecting (p,d,q) for a single series live: plot → difference → ACF/PACF → candidate model → AIC/BIC comparison.

🟢 Apply

Put the concept to work in a new setting — same underlying model, a context the cohort hasn't seen yet.

Mini case5 min

A short scenario in a different industry that uses the same concept, ending in a concrete decision the cohort has to make.

Ready-to-run example
Hotel mini-case · 5 min

Occupancy forecasts show a strong December peak. What staffing or capacity decision would you recommend?

Same concept, new context: Retail → inventory · Hotel → staffing · Transport → capacity · Healthcare → resource planning
Think–Pair–Share5–7 min

Pair the mini case with peer discussion — learners think alone, compare with a neighbour, then a few pairs share out.

Ready-to-run example
Think–Pair–Share · 5 min

What would you recommend and why? (1 min think, 2 min pair, 2 min share.)

Different context5–10 min

Re-skin the exact exercise the cohort just completed into a different industry, without changing the underlying math — tests whether the concept transfers.

Ready-to-run example
Different Context · 8 min

You just modelled hotel occupancy with SARIMA. Now sketch how you'd set up (p,d,q)(P,D,Q)s for weekly retail foot traffic — what changes, and what stays the same?

🔵 Challenge

Push reasoning and judgement past the mechanics — for cohorts who are already solid on the concept.

What-if scenario5 min

Take the case the cohort just completed and change one assumption — do they still trust the same model or recommendation?

Ready-to-run example
What-If · 5 min

What if the seasonal pattern suddenly weakens next quarter? Would you still use the same model — and the same recommendation?

Multiple perspectives7–10 min

Ask the cohort to argue the same forecast from three different stakeholder seats — surfaces that "correct" depends on who's reading the number.

Ready-to-run example
Multiple Perspectives · 8 min

How might a data scientist, an operations manager, and a CFO interpret the same forecast differently?

Challenge assumptions7–10 min

Ask the cohort to name the assumptions their recommendation depends on, then pressure-test whether those assumptions actually hold.

Ready-to-run example
Challenge Assumptions · 8 min

What assumptions must hold for your recommendation to remain valid? Which one are you least sure about?

Beyond the MVP nine: once these nine are routine, richer Challenge-level moves are worth adding — e.g. a systems lens ("if management acts on this forecast, what downstream consequences could occur elsewhere in the organisation?"). Left out of the MVP set deliberately: it needs more setup than a single prompt, so it's a good second-wave addition rather than a day-one default.
