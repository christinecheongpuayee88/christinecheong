Just enough theory

What are we forecasting?

Most business series can be read as three ingredients layered together — and past observations matter because nearby time points tend to look alike.

Trend

The long-run direction in the series.

Seasonality

A pattern that repeats every fixed cycle, e.g. every 12 months.

Why past matters

Nearby time points tend to look alike — that dependence is what every model here exploits.

Part 1 · SARIMA › Concept 03
Just enough theory

Why stationarity matters

Most classical models assume a constant mean, variance and autocorrelation over time. Real business series rarely start out that way.

Stationarity

The statistical property most models require.

Differencing

Subtract each value from the one before it, to remove a changing level.

Seasonal differencing

Subtract the value from one seasonal cycle ago, to remove a repeating pattern.

Part 1 · SARIMA › Concept 04
Just enough theory

From ARIMA to SARIMA

ARIMA combines three ideas. SARIMA repeats the same three ideas at a seasonal cycle.

AR (p)

Today depends on its own recent past values.

I (d)

How many times you difference to reach stationarity.

MA (q)

Today depends on recent random shocks.

Seasonal (P,D,Q)m

The same three ideas, repeated at a seasonal cycle.

Part 1 · SARIMA › Concept 05
Just enough theory

How do we judge a forecast?

A forecast is only trustworthy if it works on data the model hasn't seen.

Train / test

Fit on history, evaluate on a period the model hasn't seen.

Forecast errors

MAPE, RMSE measured on the holdout.

Residual diagnostics

Leftover errors should look like white noise (Ljung–Box).

Model comparison

Weigh AIC/BIC, holdout accuracy and residuals together — never one number alone.

Part 2 · ARIMAX › Concept 08
Just enough theory

Why move beyond ARIMA/SARIMA?

SARIMA forecasts a series using only its own past. But past values alone may not capture everything that drives the future.

A promotion, a price change or an ad campaign can shift demand in ways the series' own history won't predict. That's the gap ARIMAX is built to close.

Part 2 · ARIMAX › Concept 09
Just enough theory

External variables

ARIMAX adds outside information alongside the series' own past — variables that plausibly move the outcome and that you can measure or plan for.

Price

Changes in list or promotional price.

Advertising

Spend or impressions over time.

Promotions

Discounts, bundles, campaigns.

Events

Holidays, launches, one-off shocks.

Economic drivers

Broader market or macro indicators.

Part 2 · ARIMAX › Concept 10
Just enough theory

Current vs lagged variables

An external driver doesn't always act immediately.

Current effect

This month's advertising lifts this month's sales right away.

Lagged effect

The effect may take a month or two to show up — lagged variables capture that delay.

Part 2 · ARIMAX › Concept 11
Just enough theory

ARIMA/SARIMA vs ARIMAX

Adding external variables doesn't automatically make a better forecast — more inputs can just add noise.

Predictive relationship ≠ causal proof. The real test is whether ARIMAX improves out-of-sample forecasting over the plain SARIMA baseline, with residuals that still look like white noise. If it doesn't clear that bar, the simpler model wins.
