Module 1 — Why DI with AI

AI Needs Structure

Without structure, AI may produce common answers, generic recommendations, and unchallenged assumptions. A weak question plus weak context equals a generic answer.

Weak AI Use

Prompt: "What should we do?"
  • Generic answer
  • Fast but shallow
  • May confirm assumptions

Structured AI Use (RGCTO)

Prompt: "Given this decision context, assess evidence, compare options and explain trade-offs."
  • Contextual — grounded in the specific decision
  • Structured and decision-ready
  • Can challenge assumptions with evidence
AI needs structure to support decision-quality thinking.

Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Prompting = Structuring the Thinking Task

Prompting is not about magic words. It is about designing the work AI should do.

Weak prompt

"What should we do?"

Problem:

AI decides what context matters, what evidence to use, and what criteria to apply.

Decision-structured prompt

"Using only the supplied evidence, assess the current situation, separate observed facts from interpretations, identify evidence gaps, and avoid recommendations at this stage."

Benefit:

AI supports the current decision step without jumping ahead.

In DI, prompts should control the thinking sequence — not just the writing style. Where AI should stop matters as much as what it should do.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

RGC Prompting

Role + Goal + Context, then Output & Constraint — a versatile structure that can be applied to almost any input and desired output.

Role

ChatGPT's persona

"You are an expert marketer"
Goal

Purpose of the output

"The goal is to communicate to a group of 12 year old kids"
Context

Who, what, where and why

"The email is to educate the parents"
Output

Desired output

"Create 5 emails ending with a call to action"
Constraint

Limitations and guidelines

"The email is friendly and less than 300 words"
Prompt formula
You are an expert [role]. Create [output]. The goal is [end goal]. The content is for [context]. Your guidelines for writing are [constraints].
RGC establishes a standardised structure for producing the desired output. Treat it as a reusable structure, not a mandatory formula.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Prompt Priming — Provide Context

Relevant context grounds AI in the decision situation and reduces unsupported assumptions.

Unprimed

"How do we improve sales?"

Likely result:

  • Generic recommendations
  • Popular advice
  • Missing constraints

Primed with context

"Traffic is increasing. Conversion is declining. CSAT is falling. Complaints mention waiting time and stock-outs. Budget is constrained."

Likely result:

  • More grounded reasoning
  • Better focus
  • More useful trade-offs
Priming does not guarantee truth, but it improves the quality of AI-supported reasoning before you even ask the question.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Ask Before Answer Prompting

Guides the model to seek clarification before answering, so the response is built on what you actually meant — not a guess.

Prompt formula
You possess expertise in the field of [industry]. I will request your assistance with specific tasks, but I kindly request that, before offering your response, you follow this process: if you encounter any uncertainties or require clarification regarding my task, please ask for clarification using bullet point questions. Is this clear?

Excellent, my question is [question]. Your objective is to [task]. Prior to completing your task, please feel free to seek clarification by asking any questions you deem necessary to enhance my prompt.
A model that asks first produces fewer answers you have to throw away — an important precursor to problem framing in Module 7.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Shot Prompting

Different levels of guidance provided before generating a response — zero, one, or several examples.

Zero Shot

No previous data or guidelines before completing the request

"How can AI help with business operations?"
One Shot

One piece of data or a single guideline given first

"How can AI help with customer retention for mid-sized e-commerce companies in Southeast Asia?"
Few Shots

Multiple pieces of data or several guidelines given first

"I'm preparing a strategic note on leveraging AI for customer retention... churn prediction, personalised offers, AI-powered loyalty programs. Can you provide examples with measurable outcomes?"
More shots ground the output in your specifics — at the cost of a longer prompt. Useful when the desired format or style is hard to specify in words.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Tabular / Structured Output Prompting

A structured approach that has the model present its answer in a well-organised format — a table, a ranked list, or any structure you name — using categories you choose.

Prompt formula
[Your question]. Identify distinct categories to further elaborate on your answer. Create a table that presents your original response with these categories as separate columns.
Worked example
What are some AI tools that mid-sized companies in Singapore can use to improve internal productivity? Identify distinct categories such as Tool Name, Function, Use Case, Pricing Tier, Integration Difficulty. Create a table that presents your original response with these categories as separate columns.
Naming the columns yourself keeps the output decision-relevant, not decorative — structure makes evidence easier to compare and inspect.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Chain / Multiple Explanations for Brainstorming

Chain of Thought asks the model to reason step by step. Extended for brainstorming, it explores several possible explanations before narrowing.

Chain of Thought

One line of reasoning, shown step by step

Prompt formula
[Your question]. Let's break it down step by step.
Multiple Explanations

Several possible explanations, generated and compared

Prompt formula
[Your question]. Generate three distinct approaches. For each, reason through the trade-offs step by step. Then compare all three and recommend the strongest, explaining why.
CoT gives you one strong line of reasoning. Exploring multiple explanations gives you several to compare before narrowing — a preview of the Thinking Partner role in Module 8.
Module 2 — Prompting for Decision Work

Perspective Prompting

A framework for a broader, more nuanced understanding of a topic — enabling more informed decisions.

Singular Perspective
Prompt formula
Kindly provide insights on [topic] from the viewpoint of [viewpoint].
Multiple Perspectives
Prompt formula
Please present arguments, both in favor and against the topic of [topic], incorporating various diverse viewpoints. Include the names and viewpoints of these perspectives, such as [viewpoints].
Naming the viewpoints by role — not just "pros and cons" — surfaces sharper disagreement. A preview of the Decision-Support Partner role in Module 9.

Module 3 — Grounding, Evidence & Tools

Internal Evidence vs External Context

Internal and customer evidence show what is happening inside. Market and competitor research provide external context.

Internal Evidence

What is happening inside

  • Performance and transaction data
  • Customer feedback and complaints
  • Operational records
External Context

What is happening in the market

  • Market and industry trends
  • Customer research
  • Competitor moves
Both matter — but they answer different questions. Internal evidence shows what happened here; external context shows what's happening around you.

Module 3 — Grounding, Evidence & Tools

External Context ≠ Internal Cause

External signals can support plausibility. Only internal evidence can establish what happened here.

External signal alone

"Industry-wide churn is rising, so that must be why our numbers dropped."

Problem:

An industry pattern makes an explanation plausible — it does not confirm it happened in your organisation.

External + internal evidence

"Industry churn is rising, and our own complaint data shows the same pattern — together this supports the explanation."

Why it holds up:

The external signal provides context; the internal evidence provides proof.

Treat external evidence as contextual, not as proof of internal cause. Confirming what happened here always requires internal evidence.

Core Prompt 1 — Problem-Framing Coach

Use this prompt after your team has attempted its own framing — AI checks and refines, it does not frame from scratch.

Problem — Is it clear what needs investigation and why it matters? 2. Decision — Is it clear what decision the analysis should support? 3. Gaps — Is it clear what important information is still missing? Identify what is already clear and what should be improved. Module 7 — From Prompt Chain to Workflow

Workflow Trap: When AI Does Too Much

If AI frames, explains and decides while the human only accepts, efficiency becomes cognitive offloading.

The trap

AI frames→ AI explains→ AI decides→ Human accepts

Fast, but the human has become a rubber stamp — not a decision-maker.

The workflow

AI assists framing→ AI assists explaining→ AI assists deciding→ Human judges throughout

Slower per step, but the human stays the decision-maker at every stage.

Efficiency is only safe when a human checkpoint survives at every stage — not just at the end.
