How It Works
Five-agent pipeline

Each agent has a distinct role. Together they form a complete adaptive teaching workflow.

1
Learner Insights Agent
Who is in the room?

Analyses survey responses to build a cohort profile — learner background, prior knowledge, confidence levels, goals, interests, sector context and common challenges. Produces the cohort intelligence brief that all downstream agents use.

Experience mix Dominant goals Knowledge gaps Sector context Discussion themes
2
Teaching Orchestrator Agent
What to emphasise, simplify, skip or extend?

Turns cohort intelligence into an adaptive teaching playbook. Recommends pacing changes, concept prioritisation, which slides to linger on versus skip, and how to sequence activities for this specific cohort. The instructor's session plan, rewritten for the room.

Pacing recommendations Time reallocation Concept priority order Group strategy
3
Activity Adaptation Agent
Which cases, examples and datasets fit this cohort?

Tailors the hands-on layer: which case study to lead with, which dataset feels most familiar, what sector-specific examples to substitute in, how to reframe exercises at different difficulty levels, and what stretch tasks to assign to advanced learners.

Case assignments by group Sector-mapped examples Tiered exercise variants Dataset recommendations
4
Classroom Support Agent
Live teaching support when the room needs a pivot.

Provides in-the-moment guidance during class — alternative explanations for concepts that are landing poorly, group discussion prompts calibrated to the cohort's sector and experience, quick pivot activities when energy drops, and challenge questions for learners who are ahead.

Discussion prompts Alternative explanations Pivot activities Challenge questions
5
Reflection & Transfer Agent
Closes the loop. What did learners actually take away?

Generates personalised end-of-class reflection prompts, workplace application tasks matched to each learner's role and sector, and instructor improvement notes for the next cohort. Connects what was learned to what learners will actually do differently at work.

Personalised reflections Workplace transfer tasks Goals vs outcomes check Instructor debrief notes
Worked Example
Decision Intelligence course

The AI Adaptive Classroom generalises across courses. Here is how it would configure a Decision Intelligence session.

Survey Signal

Cohort is mostly beginners who struggle with problem framing

11 respondents · avg experience 2.3/5 · dominant gaps: structuring the decision, knowing what to predict

What the Learner Insights Agent found

Most learners can describe a business problem but cannot yet convert it into a predictive question. They know what decisions they make — they don't know how to frame data requirements around them.

Teaching Orchestrator
  • Spend more time on problem framing
  • Use simpler decision cases
  • Provide starter hypotheses for exercises
  • Slow down the analytics taxonomy slide
Activity Adaptation
  • Lead with familiar sector datasets
  • Mix groups by experience level
  • Assign tiered notebook tasks per level
  • Replace generic cases with sector-mapped ones
Classroom Support
  • Use Persona Perspectives during trade-off discussions
  • Checkpoint: "What is ONE thing your org forecasts by gut feel?"
  • Pivot activity if energy drops post-lunch
Reflection & Transfer
  • Different prompts for students vs working professionals
  • 30-day workplace action per participant
  • Instructor note: beginners need framing scaffold next time
Final Positioning
What this is — and is not

The distinction matters for how it is introduced to instructors and institutions.

Not this

A survey analyser that summarises responses into themes and averages. A tool that generates content based on a topic. An AI that coaches individual learners through material.

This

An AI-enabled teaching workflow that helps instructors understand the cohort, adapt the learning experience, and improve learning transfer — before, during and after class.

AI Adaptive Classroom treats the instructor as the primary user. The survey is the input. The adapted teaching session is the output. The goal is not to describe learners — it is to help instructors teach better for this room, right now.
